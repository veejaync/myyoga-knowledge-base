A beginning to the practice

What is Surya Namaskar?

Surya Namaskar is commonly translated as “Salutation to the Sun.” The Sanskrit name joins Surya (“Sun”) and Namaskar (“salutation” or “greeting”). It links postures with breath and mindful attention, and can be a way to begin a yoga session with steadiness and awareness.

There is no single version used by every tradition: pose sequences, pacing, and mantras can differ. The 12 steps below follow the sequence in the supplied table.

Movement · breath · awareness

Why practise Surya Namaskar?

People practise for different reasons. The experience depends on the version, pace, frequency, adaptations, and the individual.

Movement and physical practice

The sequence moves through standing, balancing, folding, and floor-based shapes. Practised comfortably, it can warm the body and involve the arms, shoulders, trunk, back, hips, and legs.

Over time, an appropriately paced practice may support strength, mobility, coordination, and stamina. These outcomes are not guaranteed and vary from person to person.

Breath and attention

Linking a movement with an easy inhale or exhale can help build awareness of breath and body. There is no need to take oversized breaths or hold the breath to complete the sequence.

Mindfulness and meaning

Repeating a familiar sequence may offer a steady focus and a moment to pause. In some traditions, practitioners also use the practice as an expression of gratitude to the Sun and the natural rhythms of life; this meaning is personal and optional.

In simple terms: Surya Namaskar can be approached as a moving meditation—one that brings movement, breath, and attention together.

Finding a place in your practice

Ways to use the sequence

A morning practice

Some people practise at sunrise as a daily ritual. Sunrise is not required; choose a time and setting that feel comfortable for you.

A gentle warm-up

Slow, modified rounds can introduce movement before other poses. Let your body guide the range and pace rather than pushing through discomfort.

A short mindful sequence

A few rounds can be a compact movement practice, but Surya Namaskar does not replace every aspect of yoga or meet every person's needs on its own.

Adapt the practice to the person

Who can practise?

Many people explore adapted forms, but age or fitness level alone cannot determine whether a particular sequence is suitable. Beginners can start slowly, use support, and learn from a qualified teacher.

Choose a suitable version

  • Beginners may practise fewer steps or use simpler options.
  • Children should practise with appropriate supervision.
  • Older adults and people with limited mobility may prefer chair or supported variations.
  • Athletes and experienced practitioners can still benefit from adapting pace and range to the day.

When to seek guidance

Ask a qualified healthcare professional or appropriately trained yoga teacher about modifications if you have high blood pressure, a heart condition, recent surgery, significant back pain, wrist or knee problems, vertigo, or another health concern. Stop if a movement causes pain, dizziness, or difficulty breathing.

Pregnancy: The sequence may need substantial changes as pregnancy progresses. Get advice from your prenatal healthcare professional and a qualified prenatal yoga teacher; do not rely on a general sequence as pregnancy-specific guidance.

The practice · step by step

The 12 steps

Move at an easy pace. Add each photo to images/surya-namaskar/ as step-01.jpg or step-01.png (and so on through step 12). Either format works; treat the breath cues as suggestions.

Step 01

Pranamasana

Prayer Pose

Quick instruction
Stand tall with your feet grounded. Bring your palms together at your heart and let your shoulders soften. Breathe naturally.
Mantra
Om Mitraya Namah
Solar aspect
The Friend of All
Simple meaning
The Sun shines equally on everyone without discrimination.
Step 02

Hasta Uttanasana

Raised Arms Pose

Quick instruction
Inhale and sweep your arms up. Lengthen through your sides and add only a gentle upper-back arch if comfortable.
Mantra
Om Ravaye Namah
Solar aspect
The Shining One
Simple meaning
The Sun radiates light and energy.
Step 03

Padahastasana

Forward Fold

Quick instruction
Exhale and hinge forward from your hips. Soften your knees and let your hands rest on your legs, a support, or the floor.
Mantra
Om Suryaya Namah
Solar aspect
The Dispeller of Darkness
Simple meaning
The Sun removes darkness and brings clarity.
Step 04

Ashwa Sanchalanasana

Low Lunge

Quick instruction
Inhale and step your right leg back. Lower the back knee if useful, steady your stance, and lift your chest comfortably.
Mantra
Om Bhanave Namah
Solar aspect
The Illuminator
Simple meaning
The Sun illuminates the world and our understanding.
Step 05

Phalakasana

Plank Pose

Quick instruction
Step back into a stable plank with your hands under your shoulders. Keep breathing naturally; lower your knees for support.
Mantra
Om Khagaya Namah
Solar aspect
The Sky Traveller
Simple meaning
The Sun moves across the sky, marking time and seasons.
Step 06

Ashtanga Namaskara

Eight-Point Salute

Quick instruction
Exhale and lower your knees, chest, and chin toward the floor with control. If this feels uncomfortable, lower your whole body instead.
Mantra
Om Pushne Namah
Solar aspect
The Nourisher
Simple meaning
The Sun nourishes all life, including plants, animals, and people.
Step 07

Bhujangasana

Cobra Pose

Quick instruction
Inhale and slide forward. Lift your chest gently, keeping your elbows soft and shoulders away from your ears.
Mantra
Om Hiranyagarbhaya Namah
Solar aspect
The Golden Cosmic Womb
Simple meaning
The Sun is the source of creation and universal life energy.
Step 08

Adho Mukha Svanasana

Downward Dog

Quick instruction
Exhale and lift your hips into an inverted V. Bend your knees as needed and keep the pose comfortable.
Mantra
Om Marichaye Namah
Solar aspect
The Lord of Rays
Simple meaning
The Sun's rays bring warmth, growth, and vitality.
Step 09

Ashwa Sanchalanasana

Low Lunge

Quick instruction
Inhale and step your right foot forward between your hands. Lower the back knee if helpful and lift your chest comfortably.
Mantra
Om Adityaya Namah
Solar aspect
The Cosmic Son of Infinity
Simple meaning
The Sun represents divine power and universal order.
Step 10

Padahastasana

Forward Fold

Quick instruction
Exhale and bring your feet together. Fold from your hips, bending your knees as needed.
Mantra
Om Savitre Namah
Solar aspect
The Stimulator
Simple meaning
The Sun awakens life, inspiration, and creativity.
Step 11

Hasta Uttanasana

Raised Arms Pose

Quick instruction
Inhale and rise smoothly, sweeping your arms up. Lengthen upward and stretch back only slightly, if comfortable.
Mantra
Om Arkaya Namah
Solar aspect
The Radiant One
Simple meaning
The Sun is a source of brilliance, health, and purification.
Step 12

Pranamasana

Prayer Pose

Quick instruction
Exhale and bring your palms together at your heart. Stand steady and notice your breath.
Mantra
Om Bhaskaraya Namah
Solar aspect
The Giver of Light and Wisdom
Simple meaning
The Sun bestows knowledge, awareness, and enlightenment.

Pause and reflect

After the sequence, notice which transitions felt smooth, where you chose to adapt, and how your breath felt. This sequence is a learning guide; practice at your own pace and seek qualified guidance if you are unsure about a movement.
