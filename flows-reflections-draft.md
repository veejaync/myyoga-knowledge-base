# Flows & Reflections

> **Editorial draft for review — not yet published.** This draft brings the existing lesson outline together with the handwritten sequence notes in `Inputs/`. A few pose names, transitions, sides, and hold counts are unclear in the source images and are marked for confirmation.

## Purpose

Explore yoga flows as linked movements and comfortable breaths. Begin with simple mobility, move into a chosen sequence, then pause to notice what happened. The aim is observation, not completing every pose or matching a fixed pace.

## Before you begin

- Choose a comfortable range of movement and keep the breath natural; do not force or hold it.
- Treat the inhale and exhale cues below as suggestions from the notes, not strict timing.
- Use a wall, chair, or other support when helpful. Skip or adapt any movement that does not feel suitable.
- Pause and return to normal breathing if you feel pain, dizziness, or feel unwell.
- The sequences are learning notes, not individualized instruction or medical advice. Seek qualified guidance when you are unsure whether a pose is appropriate for you.

## Suggested order for a practice

### 1. Arrive and warm up

Start in a comfortable position and notice a few natural breaths. The notes suggest these gentle mobility options:

1. The notes list **Rainbow** as a warm-up, but do not describe the movement.
2. From a Half-Table position, explore Cat and Cow, Thread the Needle, Balancing Table, Dancer's Table, Child's Pose, and Three-Legged Dog.
3. The note beside this Half-Table series says “15 counts each; repeat the other side.” Confirm what the count applies to before using it.

Practise balancing variations only if steady and comfortable. Include Three-Legged Dog only if Downward-Facing Dog is comfortable; repeat on the other side.

Other warm-up ideas in the notes are **Malasana Twist**, **Drop the Knee**, **Single-Leg Twist**, and **Single-Leg Out Push**. These are names only; clarify the intended movements before publishing step-by-step directions.

One page also lists transitions from Mountain Pose toward Pigeon, Lizard, and Warrior poses, without specifying the route. Treat these as ideas to clarify rather than a fixed sequence.

Another all-fours preparation is written as:

All Fours → lift the right leg → hold it with the left hand → bring the right leg between the hands → raise the right hand → lower the knee → Three-Legged Dog → knee toward forehead → Warrior I → Flying Lizard → Lizard → Plank → Puppy Pose → Cobra Pose → Child's Pose → Rabbit Pose → Child's Pose.

The hand/leg hold and the transition from Lizard to Plank are unclear in the source. Keep this as a transcription to confirm, not a follow-along instruction.

### 2. Choose a flow

The following are separate sequence ideas from the notes, not one continuous routine. Choose one at a time and take a resting pose between sequences.

#### A. Hip-opening flow

This is the most fully labeled sequence in the handwritten notes. Move slowly and use support for the hands if needed.

1. Tabletop → Downward-Facing Dog.
2. Step the right foot forward to Lizard Lunge.
3. Explore lowering the elbows only if comfortable; the notes also indicate lifting the right hand and then lowering it.
4. Continue through the noted Lizard and lower-lunge variations.
5. Move toward Pigeon Pose, with the chest open only as comfortable.
6. Return through Lizard and a forward-folding variation, then return to Downward-Facing Dog.
7. Repeat on the left side.

The source notes say “10 count each,” but do not make clear which poses this applies to. Do not treat this as a required hold; confirm the intended count before publication.

The same page adds a standing hip sequence:

1. Goddess Pose → High Lunge.
2. Move through Skandasana to one side, then to the other side; one note mentions lifting the left hand.
3. Continue to a seated side-angle variation (written as “Parivrtta Janu Sirsasana”).
4. Repeat on the other side and return to Goddess Pose.
5. Explore a Half Bend and a forward-bending Goddess variation.

The notes suggest “hold each posture for 20 counts.” Confirm whether that count applies to this sequence and whether it is intended as a breath count or a timed hold.

#### B. Seated mobility flow

From Staff Pose (Dandasana), the notes outline a seated side-body sequence:

1. Dandasana → lift the right leg and pause.
2. Explore a seated twist.
3. Bring the knee toward the side and lower it into a side-angle variation (the notes name Parivrtta Janu Sirsasana).
4. Move into Janu Sirsasana.
5. Explore Gate Pose on both sides.
6. Forward fold → Reverse Tabletop → return to a forward fold.

The original inhale/exhale marks are not consistent for every transition. Let the breath remain easy rather than trying to fit the movement to a forced count.

#### C. Standing balance and strength flow

The notes give this standing sequence:

Chair Pose (the note adds “on the toes”) → One-Legged Mountain Twist → Runner's Lunge → Warrior II → forward bend → Warrior II → forward bend → Reverse Warrior → Side Plank → Lunge → cactus-arm Lunge → Pyramid Pose → One-Legged Mountain Twist → Chair Pose.

The source also says “move your hands” near the start, but does not specify the hand movement. The transitions and side changes are incomplete; practise each side evenly and confirm the intended order before publishing this as a follow-along sequence.

#### D. Breath-led Vinyasa sequence

The first Vinyasa page starts in Child's Pose and links a half-Camel variation with a floor sequence:

1. Child's Pose → right half-Camel variation → Child's Pose.
2. Repeat with the left side.
3. The notes then appear to cue Ashtanga Namaskara → Cobra Pose → Mountain Pose.
4. From Mountain Pose, lift the right leg; move the right knee toward the right elbow, then return the leg up.
5. Move the right leg across or underneath the body into Wild Thing, if appropriate and comfortable.
6. Continue through Warrior I → twisted Half-Moon variation → Reverse Warrior → Pyramid Pose.
7. Return through Warrior I → Warrior II → Reverse Warrior → twisted Triangle → Anjaneyasana (Low Lunge).
8. Move to Plank → Cobra Pose → Child's Pose.

Several links on this page are hard to read, including the transition after Cobra, the leg placement before Wild Thing, and the final standing-pose order. Treat this as a sequence outline to confirm—not as a finalized set of movement instructions.

#### E. Floor Vinyasa and twist variations

The notes contain two more floor-based outlines:

**Variation 1:** Downward-Facing Dog → Upward-Facing Dog → Cobra Pose → take the right leg across/outside the left → Wild Thing → Three-Legged Dog → Lunge → twisted Lunge → Anjaneyasana variation → twisted Lunge (the note mentions pressing the left hand) → forehead toward knee with the left leg forward → Padahastasana.

**Variation 2:** Sit on the knees → rotate right and left → Ashtanga Namaskara → Cobra Pose → take the right leg outside → Wild Thing → Pigeon Pose → Matsyasana (Fish Pose) → stretch/hold the leg with the opposite hand → look back → return to Pigeon → Pigeon-to-Plank transition → Cobra Pose → Child's Pose.

These are not yet clear enough to teach as written. Confirm the pose transitions, side changes, and whether “Upward-Facing Dog” and “Cobra Pose” are separate steps.

#### F. Downward-Dog leg sequence

Another page records a right-side sequence beginning in Downward-Facing Dog:

Downward-Facing Dog → knee toward elbow → Side Plank (right hand raised) → Pigeon Pose → a pose written as Matsyasana (Fish Pose) → Pigeon Pose with hands raised → Puppy Pose → Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose) → Downward-Facing Dog.

The transition between Side Plank and Pigeon, and the placement of Fish Pose within this sequence, need confirmation. The notes label this side “right leg”; add and verify the corresponding left-side version before teaching it as a balanced flow.

Another short right-leg outline reads: Downward-Facing Dog → step the right foot between the hands → Low Lunge → Skandasana → lower the knee → rotate → lift the right leg back to Three-Legged Dog. The rotation and return transition are not specified.

### 3. Cool down and pause

The notes repeatedly return to Child's Pose. Use it—or another comfortable resting position—as a pause between sequences and at the end. Allow the breath to return to its natural rhythm.

## Reflection

After practising, jot down a few observations:

- Which movements felt steady and comfortable?
- How did your breath respond as the sequence changed?
- Which transition would you like to slow down, simplify, or practise with support?
- What would you like to explore or adapt next time?

These are prompts for personal observation, not a test or a requirement.
